## Adversarial interleavings considered

1. Hold writer, queue64 ordinary frames, then peer request: the control uses the65th physical slot while ordinary excess waits; J21 proves reader progress and actual answer before matching initialization, for ping and unsupported methods.
2. Queue early answer, then supply matching initialize before writer drain: ReadyForWriter handoff waits behind the answer in the same FIFO; J22 verifies answer precedes initialized and retains pre-negotiation version.
3. Fill65 slots, then another peer request: deliver returns TooLarge and reader raises closing before end. Later close/failure cannot replace Shared's cause; queued effects face HTTP closing/failed-phase checks. J23 tests this with one DELETE and startup/body release.
4. Cancel an ordinary sender before semaphore acquisition or after acquisition behind controls: both drop ownership; dequeue frees a permit even while returned frame is held. J24 checks both and receiver loss. Dropping receiver releases buffered permits, so suspended semaphore callers can acquire then observe channel closure; no permanent permit wait remains.
5. Deadline expires with answer/handoff behind pressure: startup timeout records Recovery::Failed; check_reply_binding and validate_handoff refuse later effects/admission. J25 and existing J9/J11 cover expiry and completion-loss boundaries.
6. Explicit close or writer Unauthorized wins before answer dispatch: HTTP fence and first Shared cause prevent the queued answer/initialized effects; J26 preserves cause across later close and asserts one DELETE.
7. Receiver closes before peer try_send: Closed produces retained cause/ServerGone. Custom-exchange unwind is exercised in J23 as an actual receiver-loss neighbor, not claimed as immediate panic supervision.

No new concrete failing interleaving emerged. FIFO here is channel admission order; the design explicitly does not promise control priority or unlimited progress under control pressure.
