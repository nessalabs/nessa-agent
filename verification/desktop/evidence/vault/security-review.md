## Adversarial agreement checks

I read the actual owners and regression sources, not only earlier reports. The following related facts were traced together through admission, asynchronous work, persistence, cleanup and projection:

- **Owner, key and generation:** `nessa-vault` keeps one unique Session and cloneable non-owning Lease. Closing the owner invalidates admission before waiting for retained-key cleanup. Explicit lock advances the publication fence and removes its key in one state critical section. KDF publication checks owner activity and generation; failed publication audit clears the assigned key, and closure during audit retains compensating key-cleared evidence. Scoped read/write/delete compare the captured generation under the same key mutex. Tried the old-owner close/new-owner unlock and explicit-lock/late-KDF orderings against these owners; neither authorizes a stale publication or newer-generation entry mutation. Core lifecycle and real GitHub codec tests retain regression coverage.
- **Same-session ordering versus new-session independence:** GitHub restore, connect, cancel and disconnect capture a generation and use its command gate across IO/publication. `change_in` validates before selecting the gate and after awaiting it. A new generation selects a different gate, so an old provider request cannot block its restore. A delayed restore receipt cannot overtake same-generation disconnect, including SignedOut/Held::Nothing; a failed restore cannot overwrite a current connect. Poll end/complete take the identified Attempt's generation and revalidate operation ownership after waiting. Cancel/replacement while a terminal waits cannot make an old terminal consume the new Attempt. The two same-generation regressions and old-generation provider/load regressions cover the negative and feasible positive cases; scoped store fences independently protect durable entries.
- **Actual state, cleanup and attributed audit:** stale retirement takes the owned Attempt once, aborts its poll, and synchronously keeps the original caller/operation with VaultLocked cause. Explicit lock and socket close attempt GitHub cleanup independently of a vault key-audit failure. Terminal operations take Attempt ownership before awaiting their audit/store effects. The product regression checks cleanup before a later status read can trigger it, and the lint-only lexical scope now visibly releases evidence borrows before teardown.
- **Retained audit versus appended evidence:** `FileAudit` bounds bytes/rows, validates restored and appended rows before retention or replacement, checks legal state/action/cause/result/target combinations, initiator length and same-lease actor agreement, unique lease/operation, timestamp range and contiguous durable sequence. I checked malformed suffixes, actor contradiction and duplicated operation against the validator and tests; refusal preserves original bytes. The encrypted repository reloads under a private shared lock before atomic publication, syncs and verifies its receipt. Wrong phrase, malformed framing and corrupt ciphertext do not initialize replacement data. Secret Debug is redacted; passphrase/key/plaintext/base64 buffers use their erasing owners where Rust ownership permits. The golden ciphertext fixture covers the fixed format/KDF profile. No assertion of forensic heap erasure or high entropy for every user-chosen phrase follows from a length floor.

The private marker can cause unavailable recovery if locally corrupted; it does not decrypt data or silently reset missing keys.
