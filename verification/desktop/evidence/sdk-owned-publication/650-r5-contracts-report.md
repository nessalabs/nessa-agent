## Same-head R5 amendment: accepted resource binding can terminate the owned root drain

**MAJOR / P1 — genuine PR650 race.** Independently source-traced external CodeRabbit comment4208349773 on unchanged clean3e65d96e. This is an amendment of cumulative round5, not a new round or budget reset. No execution, mutation, repository edit or build was performed. Earlier no-findings statements describe the initial inspection outcome and are superseded by this finding.

Primary locations: `crates/nessa-sdk/src/application/agent_execution/subagents/root.rs:124` converts an atomic absence refusal caused by a winning resource transfer into `OwnershipFailure::Incomplete`; `coordinator.rs:1439` propagates that result with `?`, terminating the owned drain. The authoritative transfer is `coordinator.rs:340–355`.

Concrete allowed interleaving, with healthy audit/store ports throughout:

1. A successful root admission queues its DeliveryTicket, and the caller drops the unclaimed open_root future. Its captured live executor starts reconciliation, atomically retaining/sealing the root and starting the ordinary owned drain. This produces an eligible Closing root without an owner or absence claim.
2. `drive_drain` obtains that Closing root as a target. `claim(root)` at1428 returns None because the resources map is empty. Its preliminary `root_eligible` and `!bound.contains(root)` check at1429–1436 succeeds outside the admission scope.
3. Before settle_never_bound acquires the admission scope, another thread calls public bind_resources with an actual cleanup owner. This is legal Closing binding: eligibility holds, there is no factory flight, existing owner, physical release or absence claim. The binding wins the admission scope, sets bound, stores the exact owner, returns Ok, and notifies. This interleaving is reachable with multi-thread concurrency even though the drain has no intervening await.
4. `settle_never_bound` now acquires the scope. Its authoritative recheck atroot.rs107 observes bound=true and correctly refuses to fabricate physical absence by returning Ok(None). At124 that ordinary race outcome becomes Err(Incomplete).
5. The `?` atcoordinator.rs1439 ends drive_drain immediately. It never executes another resources claim or the accepted owner's close. start_drain stores the completed Incomplete result; the binding notification cannot restart a drain that has returned. The unclaimed-delivery reconcile path has no caller waiting on that result and schedules no automatic retry. The root remains Closing with the accepted cleanup owner retained but unclosed until a separate explicit close is initiated.

A visible end_lifetime caller could retry, so this is not an assertion that explicit recovery is impossible. The defect is that a transfer which legitimately wins the atomic absence race stops the already-owned cleanup task, particularly when the original caller is gone. A notify is not an owner of a later retry. No fallible audit/storage port, restored identity uncertainty, child completion, blocking adapter or panic is needed.

This violates canonical row22: “Resource binding wins admission scope before absence claim … normal close drains it,” and the new root-delivery reconciliation claim that the owned transaction handles unclaimed success. Row21's refusal safety is preserved, but safety alone does not enforce the inverse winner's drain progress. The inspected rows22/23 tests bind before invoking close; they do not force binding between the drain's preliminary observation and the scoped absence recheck. Other row24/26 gate/owner tests also do not cover this boundary.

The new settle_never_bound/drive_drain branch and its propagated Incomplete create this failure. It differs from the previously adjudicated terminal-write debt: there is a known accepted physical owner in this process and all persistence succeeds. #646 settlement-write repair, #649 unknown restored absence/child completion and #625 panic/cache supervision do not excuse abandoning that known cleanup owner after losing the absence race. It is a scoped new concurrency defect and a genuine major finding.

Suggested correction for a future owner: represent “a transfer won; no absence claim made” as a non-error outcome for the drain to rescan/claim the actual owner, or atomically select owner-versus-absence under one admission authority. Preserve typed actual audit/store failures separately. The ordering table and a deterministic test should cover accepted binding specifically between the preliminary empty/unbound observation and the scoped recheck, through unclaimed root delivery with owner close/cause/settlement observations. This reviewer has not implemented or tested a repair.

Current final R5 disposition: one open MAJOR/P1 finding; no other open findings. Root decides the bounded-round handoff. Pending gates and prior passing suites do not negate this reachable ordering.
